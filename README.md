# Project A: Visulizing Air Pollution Data
## Community Action Computing, Westmont College 
#### Prof. Mike Ryu *mryu@westmont.edu*

*** 

This project is part of my Community Action Computing class and focuses on visualizing air pollution data. Specifically, it explores PM2.5 levels across California to better understand air quality trends and their potential impact on communities.
***

### The Story
As a college student in California with friends from various cities across the state,
I am deeply concerned about the alarming levels of PM2.5 pollution, which pose serious health risks and highlight 
the urgent need for stronger environmental policies reguarding vehicle emissions.

### Context 
As I read through [the article](https://www.iqair.com/us/usa/california/los-angeles) that inspired the prework for this project,
I began to wonder how the air quality trends and pollutants in Los Angeles fit into the larger picture of California as a whole. 
I was curious whether this specific pollutant—PM2.5, composed of small particles—was prevalent in other parts of the state and if other 
densely populated cities faced similar challenges. 
Additionally, since the article mentioned that PM2.5 levels tend to be higher in the summer months, I wanted to explore how these levels fluctuate throughout the year.

### The Data 
The dataset consists of PM2.5 measurements across California, broken down by county. I obtained this data from the official U.S. government website on air quality: 
[EPA Otdoor Air Quality Data](https://www.epa.gov/outdoor-air-quality-data). 

### SwD Strategies 
- **Color**: I used color to create a heatmap, making variations in PM2.5 levels more visually intuitive. I considered making the button green but decided against it to avoid drawing attention away from the graph (p. 120).
- **Heatmap**: The heatmap takes the shape of California to provide geographic context. Using varying saturation of the same hue effectively highlights differences in data (p. 42).
- **Simple Text**: I kept text minimal in my slideshow, allowing the visuals to take center stage while leaving detailed explanations for my verbal presentation (p. 38).
 **Storyboarding**: Mapping out the flow of my story helped shape the structure of my visualization. This process led me to use a slider to display monthly trends (p. 31-33).
- **Big Idea**: I used this technique to develop a clear thesis and build my three-minute story around it (p. 31).
